Vent

A place for thoughts you do not want to keep

I designed and built Vent around one deliberately temporary interaction: write something, send it into a black hole, and watch it disappear. There is no journal history, no AI reply, and no account waiting on the other side.

Why I built it

Not every thought needs to become a record.

I often feel better after saying something out loud or writing it down, even when nobody responds. The useful part is getting the thought out of my head for a moment. Most digital tools turn that moment into something persistent: a journal entry, a chat history, a mood log, or another thing to revisit.

The design constraint

Vent had to feel complete without storing anything. The interaction needed enough weight and finality that pressing send felt different from simply clearing a text field.

Design decisions

Restraint became the feature set.

The easiest way to weaken Vent was to make it more useful in conventional product terms. I kept asking whether a feature supported the act of letting go or quietly turned the app into something else.

No history

A sent vent is not added to a feed, timeline, archive, or review screen. The absence of retrieval is part of the interaction.

No AI response

Advice, interpretation, and reassurance would change the experience from monologue to conversation. Vent intentionally does not answer back.

No emotional scoring

There are no streaks, mood ratings, dashboards, or progress systems. A difficult moment does not become another metric to manage.

No interruption

Support links and information live away from the main interaction. Nothing competes with the input and the void.

Motion as meaning

A fade would have been technically enough. It was not experientially enough.

The animation had to communicate increasing gravitational pull. I tuned the movement to begin calmly and accelerate sharply near the void, with subtle distortion close to the event horizon and a crisp disappearance rather than a slow dissolve.

The black hole itself is drawn on Canvas with a dark event horizon, cool lensing halo, sparse orbiting particles, and a short impact ripple. Idle motion stays quiet; the scene only becomes more active when the user sends something.

I do not want to carry this.

Privacy as product behavior

The interface promises disappearance, so the implementation should behave the same way.

Vent does not intentionally upload or persist vent content. Draft text lives in browser memory while the user is typing. On send, the text moves into temporary animation state and is removed once the animation finishes.

I also avoided storing vent content in localStorage, sessionStorage, IndexedDB, cookies, analytics, or a backend. The privacy copy stays precise rather than claiming control over things outside the app itself, such as a user's keyboard, browser extensions, or operating system.

Build and delivery

Small product, real implementation.

I kept the architecture intentionally light. The product does not need a database or account system, so there is no backend in the core vent flow.

Reflection

The useful lesson was knowing when to stop.

Vent became a useful exercise in designing around one emotional moment instead of a long feature list. The difficult work was not adding functionality; it was making a tiny interaction feel deliberate enough that the product did not need anything around it.

If real use shows a recurring need, I can revisit the product. Until then, keeping it small is part of the design rather than a missing roadmap.
