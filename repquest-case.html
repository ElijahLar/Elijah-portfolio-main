Why I built RepQuest

Fitness data is useful while you log it, but it often ends in a chart or streak. I built RepQuest to give that progress another use: developing a companion and supporting a separate RPG.

Keeping fitness useful

Starting a workout, checking activity, logging a meal, or reviewing recovery has to stay quick. Those actions live in the main navigation; the companion and battle systems respond without taking over the fitness flow.

The fitness app comes first

RepQuest combines workouts, movement, meals, recovery, and insights in one app. Each tool remains useful even if you ignore the RPG.

The game reacts to the fitness app, not the other way around. People log and review health information through familiar tools first; RepQuest then translates it into XP, mastery stats, and battle readiness.

Your companion grows with your training

Fitness activity earns XP and develops Power, Agility, Recovery, and Focus. Levels unlock new forms; balanced mastery unlocks the Divine form.

How fitness powers the RPG

XP and mastery stats carry into a turn-based campaign. I designed battles to give fitness progress a playable outcome beyond another chart, streak, or badge.

Choose a move

Enemy intent appears before each turn, so you can attack, heal, buff, or debuff based on what comes next.

Prepare your team

Choose your active companion and team before starting an encounter.

Choose a route

Campaign routes branch between battles, camps, and elite encounters before reaching a boss.

Fitness drives character progression. Battles turn XP, stats, team composition, and abilities into playable decisions.

Where RepQuest is now

RepQuest is the most complete product I have built so far. I designed the fitness experience, progression, and battle systems, then developed the iOS app in Flutter.
